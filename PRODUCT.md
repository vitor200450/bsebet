# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Brazilian and international Brawl Stars esports fans, mostly mobile-first competitive viewers who follow match days, brackets, team news, and community discussion through BSEN. They open BSEN Pickems before and during tournament windows to make quick predictions, compare picks with friends, track results, and climb community leaderboards.

The context is public, social, and tournament-driven. Users need fast confidence: understand the matchup, choose a winner, see whether the prediction was saved, then return later to check performance without any financial framing.

## Product Purpose

BSEN Pickems is the public community prediction experience for Brawl Stars Esports News. It turns official esports schedules and community hype into a safe, competitive, non-monetary pick'em game.

Success means fans can:

- Discover active tournaments and match days quickly.
- Submit match predictions with clear status feedback.
- Follow brackets, scores, and outcomes without confusion.
- Compare performance on leaderboards as bragging rights only.
- Trust that the experience is aligned with esports community values and avoids gambling-coded language, mechanics, and visual cues.

## Brand Personality

**Sharp, Competitive, Newsroom-Hype.**

- **Voice:** Direct, energetic, informed, like a BSEN match-day desk host who knows the scene and keeps fans locked in.
- **Tone:** Competitive and celebratory, never manipulative. The product should make predictions feel like community participation, not risk-taking.
- **Emotional goals:** Users should feel the energy of a live esports broadcast plus the credibility of an esports news source: informed, included, hyped, and proud to back their teams.

## Anti-references

- No gambling-coded language, mechanics, or visuals: no odds, stakes, cash framing, casino styling, flashing gold, urgency pressure, risk language, or reward loops that imply financial gain.
- No generic SaaS or corporate dashboard feel.
- No gray-on-gray minimalism that loses Brawl Stars broadcast energy.
- No childish toy-like UI. Playful is welcome, but the final result must stay sharp, credible, and esports-forward.
- No visual dependency on official Supercell assets in ways that imply endorsement beyond compatible esports-community styling.

## Design Principles

1. **Community Pickems, Not Risk:** Every interaction frames predictions as fandom, knowledge, and friendly competition. Copy must use terms like "pick", "prediction", "palpite", "placar", and "leaderboard", never financial or gambling-coded vocabulary.
2. **BSEN Credibility:** The experience should feel connected to a trusted esports news source: clear match context, readable status labels, and confident editorial hierarchy.
3. **Broadcast Energy:** Screens should feel like competitive broadcast overlays: bold type, decisive color, sharp panels, and match-day rhythm.
4. **Clarity Under Pressure:** Fans often make picks quickly on mobile. Team names, match status, lock times, saved state, scores, and winner states must be instantly scannable.
5. **Bragging Rights Only:** Celebrate correct predictions, streaks, and leaderboard rank as community achievements without implying monetary value.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG AA contrast or better.
- Preserve explicit text/background contrast on all badges, cards, buttons, tabs, and match panels.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; all animations must have non-motion equivalents.
- Keep tap targets mobile-friendly and primary flows usable one-handed.
- Avoid manipulative urgency, fear of missing out, or pressure copy.
- Portuguese (Brazil) and English must both be first-class. All user-facing strings must use the i18n system.
